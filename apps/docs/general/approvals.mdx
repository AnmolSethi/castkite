There are two kinds of approval in Castkite: an approval workflow inside your
team, and a preview link for people outside it.

## Require approval before publishing

**Where:** Settings, **Global Settings**, **Approval Workflow**

Set **Require approval before publishing** to **Required for members** and
every post a member schedules waits as a draft until an admin approves it.
Admins and owners publish as usual.

<Steps>
  <Step title="A member schedules a post">
    It is saved as a draft and marked **Needs approval** on the calendar. It
    cannot publish yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="An admin reviews it">
    **Approvals** in the top menu lists every post waiting for review, with
    its channel, date and author.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve or reject">
    **Approve** schedules the post for its date. **Reject** sends it back to
    drafts, where the member can edit it and schedule it again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Posts a member creates through a [bulk CSV import](/general/composer/bulk-csv)
follow the same rule. Posts created with an API key do not, because the key acts
for the whole organisation rather than for one member.

## Sharing a post for client sign-off

**Where:** the Calendar, hover a post, **Preview**

Every post has a public preview page. Sending that link is how you get sign-off
without giving anyone access to Castkite.

## What the recipient sees

- The post rendered as it will appear, including media and all thread parts.
- The date it is scheduled for.
- A comment box.

They do not see your calendar, your other clients, your channels, or anything
else in your organisation. No account is required.

## Collecting feedback

Comments left on the preview page come back to you, so a review cycle looks
like:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draft the week">
    Write the posts and **Save as Draft** so nothing can publish while it is
    under review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the previews">
    **Preview** on each post, copy the link, send them to the client.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the comments">
    Feedback arrives against the post it belongs to, rather than in a thread
    of email where nobody can tell which post is which.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit and schedule">
    Apply the changes, then switch the drafts to scheduled.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## A practical pattern for agencies

Combine this with [client groups](/general/channels/customers):

1. Group the client's channels under a customer.
2. Filter the calendar to that customer.
3. Draft their week.
4. Send preview links for the posts that need sign-off.
5. Schedule once approved.

## Limits worth knowing

- A preview link is not password protected. Anyone with the URL can read the
  post and comment, so treat it as unlisted rather than private.
- A preview link is a conversation, not a state: client comments do not block
  publishing. For a post that must not go out unreviewed, use the approval
  workflow above.
